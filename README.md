# Coupons-Acceptance-Assignment
Coupon Acceptance Analysis: Understanding Consumer Behavior

Project Overview

This project examines consumer behavior and coupon acceptance using driver demographic information, lifestyle characteristics, and purchasing habits. The objective is to identify patterns among drivers who accepted bar and coffeehouse coupons and determine which characteristics may be associated with a higher likelihood of acceptance.

By comparing acceptance rates across different demographic and behavioral groups, this analysis explores how factors such as age, visitation frequency, passenger type, marital status, occupation, and income relate to coupon acceptance.

The analysis focuses on two coupon categories:

Bar coupons: Examining whether drivers who visit bars more frequently are more likely to accept bar coupons.

Coffeehouse coupons: Examining whether regular coffeehouse visitors and other demographic groups demonstrate higher coupon acceptance rates.

Key Questions

What proportion of coffeehouse coupons were accepted?

Does coffeehouse visitation frequency influence coupon acceptance?

Are drivers over the age of 25 who visit coffeehouses more than once a month more likely to accept coffeehouse coupons?

How do passenger type, marital status, occupation, and income relate to coffeehouse coupon acceptance?

Are drivers who visit bars more frequently more likely to accept bar coupons?

Findings and Analysis

1. Bar Coupon Acceptance

The overall acceptance rate for bar coupons was approximately 41%.

One important observation was that drivers who visited bars more than once a month and were over the age of 25 had an acceptance rate of approximately 62.15%. This suggests that drivers who already participate in activities involving bars may be more receptive to bar-related promotions.

Additional comparisons from Question 6 showed acceptance rates ranging from approximately 60.07% to 62.81% across the three identified groups.

These findings suggest that visitation frequency is an important characteristic associated with bar coupon acceptance. Other characteristics, including age, passenger type, marital status, occupation, and dining habits, may also contribute to differences in acceptance behavior.

2. Coffeehouse Coupon Acceptance

The overall distribution of coffeehouse coupon acceptance was nearly evenly divided:

Accepted (1): 49.92%

Not accepted (0): 50.08%

This indicates that approximately half of the coffeehouse coupons in the dataset were accepted.

Coffeehouse Visitation Frequency

Drivers who visited coffeehouses more than three times per month had an acceptance rate of 61.42%, compared with 59.91% among drivers who visited three or fewer times per month.

Although the difference was relatively small, the results suggest that frequent coffeehouse visitors may be slightly more receptive to coffeehouse coupons.

Age and Coffeehouse Visitation

Drivers who visited coffeehouses more than once a month and were over the age of 25 had an acceptance rate of 62.55%, compared with 53.87% among all other drivers.

This represents a difference of approximately 8.69 percentage points, suggesting that the combination of age and coffeehouse visitation frequency may help identify consumers who are more likely to accept coffeehouse promotions.

Additional Behavioral and Demographic Characteristics

The analysis also examined drivers who:

Visited coffeehouses more than once a month.

Had passengers who were not children.

Worked in occupations other than farming, fishing, or forestry.

This group demonstrated an acceptance rate of 64.20%.

Condition B demonstrated the highest acceptance rate at 67.08%, suggesting that younger drivers who regularly visit coffeehouses may be particularly receptive to coffeehouse coupons.

Condition A also showed a relatively high acceptance rate of 64.20%, while Condition C had an acceptance rate of 60.07%.

Overall Conclusions

The findings suggest that existing consumer habits are an important indicator of coupon acceptance. Drivers who regularly visit bars or coffeehouses generally demonstrated higher acceptance rates when compared with broader groups of drivers.

For bar coupons, drivers who visited bars more than once a month and were over the age of 25 had an acceptance rate of approximately 62.15%, considerably higher than the overall acceptance rate of approximately 41%.

Similarly, coffeehouse coupon acceptance was higher among drivers who regularly visited coffeehouses. The combination of coffeehouse visitation frequency and age appeared particularly relevant, with drivers under 30 who visited coffeehouses more than once a month demonstrating the highest acceptance rate among the coffeehouse groups examined, at 67.08%.

These observations suggest that businesses may benefit from targeting consumers whose existing habits align with the products or services being promoted. However, demographic and behavioral characteristics should be considered together rather than treated as independent explanations for coupon acceptance.

Future analysis could use logistic regression to estimate the relationship between individual demographic and behavioral variables and coupon acceptance while controlling for other characteristics. Statistical significance testing and model evaluation could also help determine whether the observed differences represent meaningful patterns.

Final Takeaway

Consumer behavior provides valuable insight into coupon acceptance. Drivers who already participate in activities associated with a particular coupon category appear more likely to accept related promotions. Understanding these behavioral patterns can help businesses develop more targeted marketing strategies, improve promotional effectiveness, and better identify potential customers.
